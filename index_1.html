About

I'm a junior at The Early College at Guilford, a dual-enrollment program where I take college-level coursework at Guilford College alongside high school. I carry a 4.0 GPA with eight AP scores of 5 — but what shapes how I think isn't the grades. It's a question I keep returning to:

How do you build a system that works in the real world, not just on a whiteboard?

That question first hit me at Open Door Ministries, where I coordinate weekly meals for 75–100 homeless residents. I designed a full scheduling platform to solve coordination failures I saw firsthand — and learned, when it wasn't adopted, that technically correct and actually useful aren't the same thing.

It came up again in FTC Robotics, debugging control loop failures at 11 PM before a competition, retuning PID parameters until the hardware and software finally agreed. And again at ThinkNeuro, building predictive models that worked cleanly in Python but needed to be rethought entirely for real users.

Currently, I'm a research intern at NC A&T's Applied Science & Technology Lab, working on synthetic bacteriophage genome engineering — and building an ECG biosensor that combines custom hardware with ML-based signal analysis. Both push me toward the same edge: where computation meets biology, where signals degrade, where systems must remain reliable under real-world uncertainty.

That edge is where I want to work.
